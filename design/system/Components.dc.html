ANCHOR STATE — ONE VOCABULARY, FOUR PLACES The card carries its own state. Its body takes a low-saturation wash of the state colour with a matching border, and the numbered token repeats that colour at full strength. Selection deepens the same wash and brightens the same border — a card never changes hue when you select it, only intensity, which is what keeps state and selection legible on one surface. State is named in words in the meta line too, so nothing rests on colour alone.
1 ok — resolved exactly. Steel fill. 2 moved — re-found after the text changed. Amber fill, never a dashed hairline. 3 resolved — outlined, drained of colour, still numbered. 4LOST orphaned — no position to draw at, so it pins to the foot of the gutter and says so. 1 active — the ring, on top of whatever state it already has. — none — not checked here — a target in a document that has not been open. Muted grey, never red, and never counted as an orphan: an orphan means the text is gone, this means nobody looked. No gutter marker either, because there is nothing on screen to point at.

THE FACT GRAPH — THREE TOPIC COLOURS, AND ONLY THREE
#2a9fb3 #9085e9 #d55181 everything else
The first categorical palette in this design, and it is computed, not chosen. A node-link graph is an all-pairs surface, and with steel, amber, red and green ruled out — colour on an edge means something there — no four hues clear the colour-blindness and normal-vision floors on the graph's ground. Three do, at the deuteranopia target exactly, which is legal only because the topic is also carried by its cluster's gravity, its name at that centre, and the legend. The fourth community folds into neutral rather than inventing a hue. Red now means three things. A lost anchor, the write-capable agent, and a contradiction. The register holds — red is what is wrong and needs your eye — and a supersede stays amber, because an old decision replaced by a new one is not a fault.
WHAT THE DOCUMENT PANE MAY AND MAY NOT RESTYLE The 620px measure and the 15/1.68 setting apply only to markdown REX renders itself — that stylesheet is REX's own. Sanitised author HTML keeps its own styles, and a <webview> URL is untouchable. For those two the pane supplies only the paper ground and the gutter, and the design must survive the document looking like anything at all.
NOT DRAWN YET Bundling the three families cost 152KB, not 400 — only the Latin subsets ship, at the five weights and one italic the design uses, loaded at document level because @font-face inside a shadow root is ignored. Still undrawn: the empty state, the truncated-tree warning, the URL-open path, the synthesis-thread builder, and the comments column reappearing over the graph while the selection panel holds something. All inherit these tokens.
